# Attorney review packet — The Free Website Co.

Prepared 2026-09-06 for a one-sitting review (target: 45–60 minutes of attorney time). Questions 16–21 concern the business name and were added after a research pass; if time is short, they are the ones worth the most.

## What the business is, in four lines

- A one-person, Los Angeles-based web shop. It builds websites for small home-services businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, landscaping) **at no charge**; the client owns the domain, code, and hosting from day one.
- Revenue comes only from optional month-to-month services: Hosted ($29/mo), Care Plan ($150/mo), Lead Capture System ($297/mo), Reputation Engine ($197/mo), and quoted one-off work. No contracts, cancel anytime, full handoff within 5 business days at no charge.
- Online payments for clients (when requested) run through the **client's own Stripe account** connected to a Stripe Connect platform operated by Long Hall Financial, the owner's holding company. Funds never touch The Free Website Co.
- Public site: https://freewebsiteco.com — terms at /terms/, privacy at /privacy/, services at /services/. The site is live; contact details are being set up.

## Questions, in priority order

**Entity and name**

1. The owner is deciding between a California single-member LLC and a sole proprietorship with a DBA. Which should be the signing party on these agreements, and does the answer change any clause? (Owner's goal: the business must be separable and sellable later, and should not be tied to the owner's personal name or to Long Hall Financial.)
2. Is "The Free Website Co." acceptable as a California fictitious business name for an LLC or sole proprietor (the "Co." abbreviation, and the word "Free" in a business name)? Any LA County FBN filing wrinkles?
3. Both agreements and /terms/ currently describe Long Hall Financial as "The Free Website Co.'s parent company." The intended structure is a separate operating entity that merely **uses** Long Hall Financial's Stripe Connect platform under an arm's-length arrangement. What should the client-facing disclosure say instead, and is a short written intercompany / platform-services agreement between the two entities needed?

**Both agreements**

4. Please supply a governing-law (California), venue, and limitation-of-liability block sized for a free build and $29–$297/month services. A cap at fees paid in the prior 12 months (and $0 → a nominal figure for free builds) is the owner's instinct; correct it if needed.
5. Unavailability clause (Free Build §8, Care Plan §5): reply clocks pause up to 10 business days for illness/emergency, then the client may cancel with everything delivered. Bless or tighten.
6. Ownership language: "You own it" and "the site's code remains yours." Does the free-build agreement need an explicit copyright assignment (code, copy, design) effective at launch, with a retained license for the owner's portfolio copy (§7)? What is the ownership position **before** launch or if the client walks away under §9?
7. Is a no-charge build agreement enforceable as drafted, or should it recite consideration (the client's intake work, the optional-services relationship, the portfolio license)?
8. Client warranty on photos, logos, and testimonials (Free Build §6): does it need an indemnity, and is the wording adequate for testimonial permission under FTC endorsement rules?

**Care plan and payments**

9. Monthly, cancel-anytime billing: what is needed to comply with California's Automatic Renewal Law (disclosure at sign-up, affirmative consent, an online cancellation path, the acknowledgment email)? The agreement promises pro-rata refunds and billing that "stops the day you cancel" — keep, change, or drop?
10. Stripe Connect (Standard accounts, client-owned): with funds never held by the owner's entity, any money-transmission or disclosure concerns in California? Is the platform-fee disclosure ("disclosed in writing before the payment page goes live, and shown on the page itself") sufficient?
11. Payment-failure handling for the care plans is not yet drafted; please propose two sentences.

**Promises made on the site**

12. "Bug fixes free for 60 days after launch" and "a bug we caused is never your bill" — does this create warranty exposure beyond the intent (defects in what was built, for 60 days), and should the agreements add an as-is / no-other-warranty sentence after it?
13. The intake form's SMS-consent checkbox (unchecked by default; the business will text only with consent): is the wording adequate under the TCPA and California law for a small business doing 10DLC-registered texting?
14. /privacy/ was drafted to CalOPPA; the owner believes CCPA thresholds do not apply. Confirm, and flag anything missing for a site whose forms collect name, email, phone, and business details from Californians.

**The name (added 2026-09-06, after a research pass)**

15. "The Free Website Co." is composed entirely of ordinary words describing the service. Our reading of USPTO's own materials puts it at the descriptive-to-generic end of the spectrum, and every other US trademark we found in the "FREE WEBSITE ___" family is dead. Is this name registrable on the Principal Register at all? If only on the Supplemental Register, or only with a Section 2(f) showing years from now, please say so plainly — the owner would rather know now than pay to find out.
16. **THE FREE WEBSITE GUYS**, US serial **97059883**, is a live registered mark in **Class 042** owned by The Free Website Guys LLC, a US company running the same business model (free website builds monetized on follow-on paid work, trading since 2020). Does "The Free Website Co." create a likelihood of confusion with it? What is the realistic exposure — a demand letter, an opposition if we ever file, or nothing?
17. **freewebsitecompany.com** is a live US-facing site launched around May 2026 with a near-identical offer, trading visibly as "Chroma Marketing Company." Does its existence change anything for us in either direction?
18. **The domain has a prior identity.** freewebsiteco.com previously hosted a business trading as "The Free Website Company" (archived captures confirm the title "The Free Website Company | #1 Web design and development company" as late as August/October 2025; the prior operator appears to have been based in India). The registration lapsed and the owner re-registered it on 2026-09-06. Does inheriting a domain whose prior occupant used a near-identical name in the same industry create any residual risk — a claim by that party, or an argument that our use is not distinct?
19. **An Australian sole trader** (Callum Ray Gracie, ABN 70 955 241 477, Sunshine Coast QLD) holds an Australian *business name* registration for "THE FREE WEBSITE CO" from 1 Nov 2025 and operates freewebsiteco.com.au. We found no trademark registration for him in Australia or anywhere. We believe this is irrelevant to a US business because trademark rights are territorial — please confirm, and flag anything that would change it (for example if we later sell into Australia, or if the business is sold to an international buyer).
20. **Given all of the above, the plan is: keep the name, never file for it, never spend money defending it, and build the sellable asset elsewhere** — the domain, the Google Business Profile and reviews, the recurring contracts, the documented process, and a separate distinctive operating brand for paid services. Is that sound, and is there anything about it that would trouble a buyer during diligence?

**Insurance (30-second opinion)**

21. The owner is quoting general liability and professional/E&O coverage. Do these agreements' promises (60-day bug window, 5-day handoff, response-time commitments) raise anything a standard tech E&O policy would exclude?
