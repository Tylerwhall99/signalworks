# First-contact openers — the 14 confirmed prospects

> Built 2026-09-06 from `docs/marketing/prospect-list.md`. Voice rules from
> `docs/marketing/outreach-templates.md` apply: no hype, no promised outcomes,
> the free build carries itself.

**Do not send any of these yet.** Four things have to be true first:

1. A real contact email and phone number must exist. `src/config.ts` still reads
   `PHONE_PLACEHOLDER` and `EMAIL_PLACEHOLDER`.
2. The intake pipeline has to actually deliver. `CONTACT.formEndpoint`,
   `CONTACT.accessKey` and `CONTACT.notifyEmail` are all empty, so the intake
   form renders a direct-email fallback instead of submitting — and there is no
   fallback address behind it either.
3. The one-page free build agreement has to clear the attorney pass and carry a
   real entity name. `docs/free-build-agreement.md` is still headed "DRAFT for
   attorney review — do not sign clients until reviewed" and its
   "Between: [LEGAL ENTITY / YOUR NAME]" line is blank. Six of the openers
   below offer to send that document by name, and four more offer to "send the
   details."
4. Every website and listing claim below must be re-checked the morning you
   send it. These were verified on 2026-09-06. A domain that was dead last week
   can be alive today, and sending someone a "your site is down" note when it is
   back up is the fastest way to look like a bot.

**What every email opener contains:** who you actually are (one person in Los
Angeles, AI-assisted, building a portfolio), that the build itself is free, that
hosting is a real cost and theirs, and when the domain, code and hosting land in
their name. All fourteen email openers carry all four. They run 109 to 138
words.

**What the phone openers leave out.** They are shorter on purpose — 76 to 94
words, roughly 30 to 45 seconds spoken, so say each one out loud once before you
dial. None of the fourteen mentions the AI tooling, and none of them says the
build's scope. Say both out loud on the call — the AI part is the honest reason
free is possible, and it should not arrive as a surprise later.

**Scope is not in any opener.** The free build is up to five pages that earn
their place, plus a privacy policy page that does not count toward the five. Say
that the moment anyone asks what "a website" means here, and let the one-page
terms carry it in writing.

**What none of them do:** claim you were a customer, claim a referral, promise
calls, leads, rankings or revenue, or open by telling someone their business
looks bad. For a dead domain the honest framing is that the address does not
resolve — a fact they very often do not know.

## Before you send

1. **Look them up yourself.** Open the listing, get the current owner name and
   phone number, and use the name they use. Three entries above have a name or
   address flagged (1, 7, 12), and a fourth has an identity question (14).
   Resolve them before you type anything.
2. **Confirm they're still trading.** Recent reviews, a recent permit, an owner
   reply, an answered phone. A business that closed doesn't need a website, and
   contacting one that has is a bad look.
3. **Re-check the website and the listing the morning you send.** Load the
   domain yourself, apex and www, http and https — and re-open the listing the
   claim rests on. Yelp, BBB and Nextdoor block automated checks, so every
   listing claim in here (Illescas's unclaimed Yelp page, Accurate's missing
   Yelp link, Air Time's Yelp website link, Leo's recent Nextdoor
   recommendations) has been eyeballed exactly once, on 2026-09-06.
   Neighborhood Roofing's Yelp page was never loaded at all — Yelp served a
   bot wall, and the listing was confirmed only indirectly — so check that
   one before you use it. If it's back up or the listing has changed, the opener is wrong —
   rewrite it or drop the prospect. Never send a stale claim.
4. **Cap it at five a day.** Five personal messages you'd stand behind beats
   fourteen that read as a blast. Space them across days so replies don't stack
   up past the one-business-day intake-confirmation clock. (Five is a judgement
   call, not a rule from anywhere in the repo — change the pace if you want a
   different one.)
5. **Log every send and every response.** One task per business in the Todoist
   project: date sent, channel, what you claimed was wrong, and the reply. If
   you can't log it, don't send it.

Two standing rules: never cite a capacity number (none exists), and never
mention another prospect to a prospect.

## What to do when they reply

**Interested.** Move to the intake, not to a pitch. Send the one-page free build
agreement — which cannot leave the building until the attorney pass is done and
a real entity name fills the blank — and the intake link, and say plainly what
happens next: a slot confirmed within one business day of intake, a first
working draft within five business days of that confirmed slot, up to five pages
that earn their place plus a privacy policy page that doesn't count toward the
five, one revision round, and 60 days of free bug fixes after launch. Say the
hosting cost again, because it is the one thing "free" doesn't cover — roughly
$0–20/month if they run it, or $29/month if you host it. State the other paid
options once, unprompted and without pressure — the Care Plan at $150/mo — and
say they're optional, month to month, with no minimum term, and that the site is
theirs either way. Then stop selling and start collecting content.

**"What's the catch?"** Answer it straight, because it's the right question.
There isn't one, and the reason is boring: AI tooling made building cheap, you
have no clients yet, and you need real work in a portfolio. You make money only
if they later choose to pay for hosting, edits or the lead tools — and plenty
won't, which is fine. Then say the part that settles it: the domain is in their
own account from day one, the code and hosting move into accounts in their name
at launch, and either of you can walk away before launch — they keep whatever
has been delivered and owe nothing. If they later take a care plan and cancel
it, the full handoff is done within five business days at no charge. There is
one thing to sign, the one-page build agreement, before any work starts — but no
monthly contract and no minimum term. Point at the one-page terms rather than
paraphrasing them. Do not add urgency, and do not sweeten the offer.

**No reply after a week.** One follow-up, short, no guilt, no second pitch: the
offer stands, the free build is still free, and if the timing is wrong that's
genuinely fine. If the website problem you flagged has since been fixed, say so
and congratulate them on it. After that follow-up, stop. Mark the Todoist task
closed and don't contact them again unless they come back to you.
