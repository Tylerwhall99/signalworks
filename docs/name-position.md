# The name: what we found, and what we're doing about it

> Researched 2026-09-06 after Tyler found freewebsiteco.com.au. Every claim below was independently re-checked by a second pass that reopened the source; 19 overstated claims from the first pass were cut or corrected. Where something could not be verified, it says so.

## Bottom line

Keep the name. Stop treating it as an asset to defend. Build the sellable asset somewhere else.

The Australian company is close to a non-problem. The research turned up three things that matter more, none of which were what we went looking for.

---

## 1. The Australian company

**Who:** Callum Ray Gracie, a sole trader (ABN 70 955 241 477), Mooloolaba, Sunshine Coast, Queensland. Not a company — no ACN, no Pty Ltd. Two visible clients. Trading under an Australian *business name* registration for "THE FREE WEBSITE CO" since 1 November 2025.

**Why it barely matters:**

- A business-name registration is not a trademark. It confers no exclusive rights even inside Australia.
- We found **no trademark registration for him anywhere** — searched IP Australia's register directly and cross-checked against TMview.
- Trademark rights are territorial. An Australian registration, if he had one, would carry no US effect.
- Everything about the operation is Australian-only: AUD pricing including GST, Queensland governing law, Australian Consumer Law clauses, and structured data naming Queensland and Australia as the only areas served. Zero US-facing content.

**The part worth knowing: their model is close to the opposite of ours.** Their own terms:

- "you are licensing the website design and code from us. We retain ownership"
- On cancellation: "We take down your website"
- "You may purchase the source code for $800 to retain ownership"
- "We control where and how your website is hosted. This is non-negotiable."
- "If we register a domain for you, we own it during your subscription"
- Six-month minimum, 50% early termination fee on the remaining balance, 30 days' notice after that

Their "free" means no money upfront, financed through a lock-in, with a takedown clause. Ours means the client owns the domain, the code, and the hosting on day one and can leave owing nothing. **Their weakest point is the exact promise our whole site is built on.** That is a competitive gift, not a threat.

## 2. What actually matters — three findings

### THE FREE WEBSITE GUYS — a live US registration in our exact class

US serial **97059883**, Class 042, owned by The Free Website Guys LLC, a US company trading since 2020 with the same model: free website builds monetized on follow-on paid work. Verified against USPTO's own records.

This is the closest thing to a real US exposure, and it is a question for the attorney rather than a reason to act. It is question 16 in the packet.

### The domain has a prior identity

freewebsiteco.com previously hosted a business trading as **"The Free Website Company"** — a name one word from ours, in the same industry. Confirmed directly from archived captures, which carry the title "The Free Website Company | #1 Web design and development company" as late as August and October 2025. The prior operator appears to have been based in India. The registration lapsed and we re-registered the domain on 2026-09-06.

**Practical consequence:** search engines may still associate that branding with the URL for a while. Nothing to do but let it re-crawl; setting up Google Search Console (already on the list) lets us ask for re-indexing directly. It is question 18 in the attorney packet.

### freewebsitecompany.com

A live US-facing site launched around May 2026 with a near-identical offer, though its visible trading brand is "Chroma Marketing Company" rather than the domain name. Question 17.

## 3. The real issue, which was always true

**"The Free Website Co." is descriptive.** It is made of ordinary words describing the service, which under USPTO's own spectrum puts it at the descriptive-to-generic end — the two weakest, least protectable categories. Every other US trademark we found in the "FREE WEBSITE ___" family is dead, which is itself evidence that names in this space do not survive registration.

Nobody in the US owns "FREE WEBSITE CO" — zero applications, zero registrations, in any class. That cuts both ways: nobody can stop us, and we cannot stop anyone. Other people will keep independently adopting some form of it, because it describes the thing.

**This is the reverse of the Signalworks situation.** That was a live US application for a distinctive coined word in our exact class in our own country. This is a descriptive phrase nobody can own. The risk of being forced into a second rename is materially lower, not higher.

## 4. The decision

**Keep the name, and demote it from brand asset to descriptive label.**

- Never file a trademark application for it. The money is better spent elsewhere and the application would likely fail.
- Never spend money defending it. There is nothing to defend.
- Keep the brand layer thin and cheap to change — which it already is, since every visible instance comes from two strings in `src/config.ts`.
- Build the sellable asset elsewhere: the domain, the Google Business Profile and its reviews, the recurring contracts, the documented delivery process, and the separate operating brand due in early October.

**This is exactly the two-brand structure already chosen.** The free-named domain is a lead-generation door that nobody owns. The operating brand is the thing that gets to be distinctive, protectable, and worth something at exit. That decision just got more important.
